Selected research

Privacy and trustworthy visual ML · Accepted at ACCV 2026 · Osaka, December 14 to 18

Anonymized but Not Anonymous: Multi-Cue Identity Leakage in Privacy-Preserving Action Recognition

Safwan Nabeel, Farah AlShiha, and Muzammil Behzad

Blurring someone’s face in a video feels like it should make them anonymous. We tested that. Using 21,600 videos, we tried to pick out the same person again after their face was hidden, relying only on what was still visible: their clothes and build, the way they stand and move, and their outline.

The blur worked on faces. Face recognition fell from 83% to 3%. But a system that recognizes people by their overall appearance still found the right person 85% of the time, almost exactly as often as with no blurring at all. Hiding a face is not the same as hiding a person.

Multimodal learning and fusion · CVPR Workshops 2026

TWASEL at SignEval 2026: Adaptive Multi-Stream Pose Fusion for Continuous Sign Language Recognition

Sadam Al-Azani, Safwan Nabeel, Qasim Al Mahfood, and Mohanad Mohamed

TWASEL separates body, hand, and facial keypoints into dedicated streams, then learns how much to use from each stream over time. On the Isharah benchmark, beam-search decoding reached 16.62% word error rate on the test set.

Reliability and distribution shift · Under review, IJCV

Are Crowd Counts Reliable? Post-Hoc Reliability Assessment for Crowd Counting Under Dataset Shift

Safwan Nabeel, Sadam AlAzani, and Muhammad Jabbar

A post-hoc framework that predicts per-image error risk for frozen crowd counters, then tests whether those estimates hold on a different dataset. Keeping the 80% of images ranked most reliable reduced mean absolute error by 19 to 68% across seven settings, though calibration did not hold up after the shift.
